the rules the text left open for these two arms and for `v3_as_built`:
- **The two arms' exact rules:**
  - `v4_btc200_shorts`: at each 00:00 decision, shorts are allowed only while BTCUSDT's
    latest daily reading (the last completed daily bar at or before the decided day) has its
    slow vote at -1, i.e. close below its SMA(200). Above, an exact tie, warming, or no BTC
    bar yet all mean not allowed. While not allowed, every coin's signal is clamped to
    `max(signal, 0)` before sizing, as an ineligible coin's is. A held short therefore
    closes at the next fill (owner decision v4-d23; but see the ruling on a held short with
    no bar, below). Longs
    keep their signals, though their sizes can still change: the clamp acts before sizing,
    and `size_book` uses one common volatility scale, the common days and the 2.7 total cap
    over the active coins, so removing the shorts can change the long targets' sizes.
  - **A held short with no bar.** The filter is a signal rule, so it does not override the
    no-bar rule (see "Signals"). A short held on a coin with no daily bar on the day the
    filter turns "not allowed" keeps its position until that coin's next bar day, when the
    clamp applies. The no-bar rule's overrides are the ones it already has (a universe exit,
    the funding-month close and, as its own rule, the dead market); the filter adds none.
  - `v4_confirm3`: a reading's direction is the sign of its raw `s` (+1, 0 or -1). Per coin,
    the arm keeps the effective signal `e` (0 before any reading), the last direction and
    its run of consecutive readings. On each new reading, the run grows if the direction
    repeats, else it restarts at 1. If the reading's direction equals `e`'s, `e` becomes the
    reading's `s` at once (a size change within one direction follows at once). Otherwise
    `e` changes to the reading's `s` only when the run reaches 3. Until then it keeps its
    value. A no-bar day adds no reading. A universe exit and a dead market still zero the
    signal at once.
  - Both arms use the main settings otherwise: 40% volatility target, main lookbacks, long
    and short.
- **`hold_btc_trend`** (rules R2 of the step-4a plan, on top of "Versions in the run"): it uses
  the same slow vote as the filter above, from the last completed BTC daily bar at or before
  the decided day. It starts in cash and makes an order only when its state changes. A
  no-bar day keeps the state. An order that never fills (refused below the filters, or
  cancelled or closed by the dead-market rule) is not made again until the vote changes.
  Its orders, fills and lifecycles carry the source and exit reason `signal`.
- **`v3_as_built`:**
  - **Window:** 2021-07-01 00:00 UTC (V3's first out-of-sample quarter) to the open of
    2024-12-31 23:00, with V3's terminal mark at 2025-01-01 00:00 as its final equity.
  - **Picks:** computed once per run with `selector_picks` (10,000 USDT, V3's size multiple
    2, base cost), written canonically and pinned by SHA-256. Every v3 cell requires that
    pin.
  - **Capital:** 1,000 USDT runs through `v3_capital`, which `replay_v3_baseline` applies
    with the disclosure `v3_capital_adapter_sets_only_starting_capital`.
  - **Open marks:** an hour's open mark is V3's `open` native mark. It is complete iff every
    one of V3's 10 coins has its hourly bar that hour. This is conservative: V3 holds a
    subset.
  - **Outcome:** status None maps to `complete`; `liquidation` maps to `liquidated`, with
    the stop at its mark's hour; every other V3 stop maps to `engine_error`, type `V3Stop`
    and message the status. So a V3 stop is incomplete, never a pass.
  - **V3Accounting:** a V3 run with status None, or a liquidation, whose
    `accounting_explained` or `wallet_quantity_exact` (V3's own reconciliation flags) is not
    true becomes `engine_error`, type `V3Accounting`, with the first failed flag (in that
    order) as its message. Such a cell is incomplete, never `complete` or `liquidated`, so
    condition 2 of the pass rule is then incomplete: the verdict relies on that comparison,
    and V3's own failed reconciliation is not valid evidence.
  - **Summary:** the format is `v4-summary-v3-1` (V4's full-summary keys, with these
    differences). `data` is `{"v3_pack_sha256": ..., "picks_sha256": ...}`, the two pins.
    `equity_2025` and `final_after_close` are null. `accounting_residual` is V3's own
    reconciliation residual (absolute value), not V4's hourly audit residual. A full summary
    (`complete` or `liquidated`) also carries `disclosures`, V3's own list of disclosures read
    from the outcome record (for a 1,000 USDT cell it includes
    `v3_capital_adapter_sets_only_starting_capital`), so that the run summary, which reads
    summaries only, can disclose the adapter as "Versions in the run" requires. The summary
    is rebuilt from the records and must equal them, disclosures included.

`.github/workflows/v4-run.yml`, in three modes (`rehearsal`, `timing` and `registered`), the

- **What the verdict reads:** each cell's `summary.json` and `receipt.json`, from one folder per
  cell named `v4-summary-<cell>-attempt-<n>`, and never a cell's records.
- **An accepted summary (V1).** A summary counts only if all of these hold. Anything else is a
  refusal, which names the check that refused it:
  - its folder names its cell, and the summary names the same cell, one of the run's;
  - its bytes are the canonical JSON of their own contents (no repeated key, no float);
  - its format is its arm's: `v4-summary-v3-1` for `v3_as_built`, `v4-summary-1` for the others;
  - it carries the run's window and data pins. A `v3_as_built` cell carries V3's window
    (2021-07-01 00:00 to the open of 2024-12-31 23:00) and the run's V3 pack and picks pins;
  - its keys are exactly its outcome's, and the outcome is one its arm can have (`v3_as_built`
    writes only `complete`, `liquidated` and `engine_error`). The reason, eras, years,
    disclosures and failure details have the shape the format gives;
  - every number is a finite decimal string, with an exponent of at most 1,000 either way, and
    the initial equity is the cell's capital;
  - its open marks run in strictly increasing hours, and the first equals its capital;
  - a full summary has its `receipt.json` beside it, which reads and hashes to the summary's
    receipt SHA-256.

  So a summary that is valid but oddly spelled is refused, and the verdict is incomplete.
- **A refused summary counts as missing (V2).** Its outcome is never read, not even for the fail
  precedence: a refused, liquidated `v4_main` gives incomplete, not fail.
- **Re-runs (V3).** A folder of an attempt other than 1 is a re-run. It is refused, and the verdict
  is incomplete whatever the cell. The same cell's first attempt still counts, so a stopped
  `v4_main` in it still fails the run. Two accepted folders of one cell refuse both.
- **The measures (V4, V5):**
  - two arms' common hourly boundaries are the hours at which both have an open mark flagged
    complete. If none remain, the condition cannot be computed, and the verdict is incomplete;
  - compounded return is the last equity over the first, minus 1, and maximum drawdown is
    `trend.metrics.maximum_drawdown` of the path, both exact. A first equity that is not positive
    cannot be computed;
  - zero drawdown follows the pass rule. "Greater" is the plain comparison, so +infinity is not
    greater than +infinity.
- **The skipped hours.** The pass rule says the hours it skips are listed. Each check of
  conditions 1 and 2 lists in `verdict.json` (`skipped_ms`) every hour open of its range that is
  not a common boundary: the run's window for condition 1, V3's period for condition 2. The page
  gives their number.
- **A liquidated `v3_as_built` in condition 2 (V6).** After its stop, the arm holds nothing. So at
  every hour after the stop, up to 2024-12-31 23:00, its equity is its final equity and its mark
  counts as complete. Only `v4_main`'s marks then decide which hours are common.
- **The conditions (V7 to V9):**
  - condition 1: the path is the common boundaries of `v4_main` and `hold_universe` (same capital,
    1x), then each arm's final equity after its window-end close. Both cells must be `complete`;
  - condition 2: the common boundaries from 2021-07-01 00:00 to 2024-12-31 23:00, both included,
    with no final point. The pass rule's "first trading hour" of `v3_as_built` is read as the
    start of its window, 2021-07-01 00:00;
  - condition 3: `v4_main` at 2x ends, after its window-end close, above its initial equity;
  - condition 4: `v4_main` at 1x ends, after its window-end close, above its equity at
    2025-01-01 00:00. If that equity is null, the condition cannot be computed.
- **Precedence (V10),** in this order:
  1. **fail** if an accepted `v4_main` summary, at either capital and either cost, is
     `liquidated`, `limit_unrestorable` or `accounting_failure`;
  2. otherwise **incomplete** if one of the 8 required cells is missing, refused or an
     `engine_error`, if a re-run or another dispatch of the same registration was found, or if a
     condition cannot be computed. The required cells are `v4_main` at both capitals and both
     costs, and `hold_universe` and `v3_as_built` at both capitals at 1x;
  3. otherwise **pass** if all 8 checks hold (4 conditions x 2 capitals), else **fail**.
- **Reported comparisons (V11),** which decide nothing. Every arm but `v3_as_built`, at each
  capital and cost, is compared with `hold_universe` by condition 1's measure and with
  `v3_as_built` by condition 2's, at the same capital and cost (`hold_universe` against itself is
  one trivial row per capital and cost). Each cell's own return, drawdown, eras and years are
  reported too, with the variants and neighbours beside `v4_main`.
- **The run summary is two pages (V12):**
  - the verdict step's `verdict.md`: the verdict and its reasons, the conditions, the comparison
    table, the eras, the variants and neighbours, the disclosures and a plain-language headline.
    It names the analysis artifact;
  - the analysis step's `diagnosis.md` (`scripts/v4_diagnose.py`): long against short, and per
    coin, from the records.

  In a rehearsal or a timing run, the verdict page (not the analysis page) opens with "Synthetic
  data: this is a test of the pipeline, not a result".
- **Resuming (V13).** The verdict step keeps a progress file, which holds numbers, never marks. It
  is replaced whole after each cell and each measure. It records the digest of the step's inputs:
  the expected file, every summary folder with the SHA-256 of its `summary.json` and its
  `receipt.json`, and the other dispatches found. A resume with other inputs is refused, and
  nothing is written. At most two cells' marks are held at once.
- **Writing the verdict (V14).** `verdict.json` and `verdict.md` are never written over. If the
  second cannot be written, the first is removed. The step exits 0 once both are written, whatever
  the verdict, and 1 when nothing is written.
- **The registration files (G1),** both canonical JSON, written in step 5:
  - `config/v4_registration.json` has exactly `schema` (`v4-registration-1`), `code_pin`,
    `run_manifest_sha256`, `spec` (path and SHA-256), `cells` (the 48 cell ids, in order) and
    `trial_id`. It never holds its own SHA-256: the dispatch passes it;
  - `config/v4_run_manifest.json` has exactly `schema` (`v4-run-manifest-1`), `data` (the data
    config's path and SHA-256, the end month, and the daily pack's and the hourly index's pins)
    and `v3_pack_sha256`.
- **The job gate (G2)** runs in the plan job, then again in every cell job, v3 cells included.
  Nothing may differ from the code pin but the two registration files,
  `docs/trials/register.jsonl` and anything under `docs/reviews/` and `docs/tasks/`. The trial
  family is `spec-v4.0`. Its completion must pin the registration, the code pin and the run
  manifest, checked as V3.1's are.
- **The rehearsal marker (G3).** `config/v4_rehearsal.json`, with fixed bytes, marks a rehearsal
  world. A rehearsal requires it at the code pin and in the working tree. A registered run refuses
  it in the working tree, at the head and at the code pin. The repository never holds it.
- **One dispatch per registration (G4).** It is V3.1's check (owner decision v31-d21), on
  `v4-run.yml`, whose runs are titled `v4-run <mode> <registration SHA-256>`:
  - the plan job of a registered run refuses when another dispatch of the same registration
    counts;
  - the verdict job checks again, first, before any download. It refuses no dispatch, but each
    one that counts, or that it cannot rule out, makes the verdict incomplete;
  - the check gives up after 20 minutes, or sooner once its job has run 25 minutes (plan) or 55
    minutes (verdict). In the plan job that is a refusal; in the verdict job it makes the verdict
    incomplete;
  - a rehearsal and a timing run only report what the check would decide.
- **The registered data (G5)** comes from a successful run of `v4-data.yml` named
  `v4-data full config/v4_data.json`, on this run's own commit. Its pins must equal the run
  manifest's, and the manifest's config file must hash to its pin. The job gate runs first, so the
  manifest is the registered one. Step 2's packs are deterministic: the data runs 39992748266 and
  40009104102 gave the same pins.
- **The workflow (W1):**
  - every artifact's name ends in `-attempt-<n>`, except `v4-data`, `v4-v3-pack` and
    `v4-rehearsal-world`. Those three are never overwritten, so re-running the whole workflow
    fails at the plan job;
  - the verdict job downloads the data pack (for the expected file's window and pins) and each
    cell's summary and receipt alone (about 3 MB a cell), never a cell's records, so it stays far
    inside its 5 GB (V3.1's aggregate died of a full disk). A rehearsal's verdict job also
    downloads the world (`v4-rehearsal-world`) and the forced cells (`v4-forced-attempt-<n>`).
    The analysis job guards its data, then downloads the V4 and hold cells' folders, never the v3
    cells';
  - the verdict and the analysis run after every cell job that ended, failed or timed out
    included, never after a cancelled run or a failed plan job;
  - if the picks job did not succeed, the expected file takes a picks pin of 64 zeros, which no
    file has. Every v3 cell then counts as missing, and the verdict is incomplete (or fail, if a
    `v4_main` cell stopped), but it is still written;
  - in a registered run the expected file refuses synthetic data; in a rehearsal or a timing run
    it requires it;
  - the verdict and the analysis (a partial one too) are uploaded once written, whatever fails
    after them. Their time and peak memory are kept even when they fail, with their exit status,
    and so are those of the verdict job's expected step, which loads the whole data pack;
  - one `v4-run` at a time: never dispatch one while another is queued or running, because GitHub
    drops a queued run when a newer one arrives. A registered dispatch is never cancelled and
    never re-run. The spec's single aggregate-only re-run is a re-run of the verdict job alone
    (with the status job), which reads the first attempt's summaries.
- **The timing run (W2)** runs the 44 cells that are not v3 on a synthetic world of the real packs'
  size (80 coins, about 7 million hourly rows), then the verdict step and the analysis over their
  outputs. Its universe changes more often than the real one, so its times err on the slow side.
  Only its timing artifacts are downloaded: the cells', the expected step's, the verdict's and the
  analysis' times and peak memory, and the cells' output sizes. It also uploads the data pack and
  the cells' folders and summaries, which its verdict and analysis read; they hold synthetic
  results only and expire after their day. The v3 cells' times come from the `v4-v3` run
  40040422092.
  - Its verdict plans no condition-2 and no v3 measures: 46 measures that load marks, against
    about 92 in a registered run. So scale its verdict time by roughly two. That scaled time, plus
    the dispatch check's deadline (55 minutes from the verdict job's start), must fit the verdict
    job's timeout of 180 minutes.
  - The verdict job's `progress/` is not uploaded, so a re-run of the verdict job alone starts
    from zero. Resuming is for a local run.
- **The rehearsal (W3)** runs the registered run's jobs on a small synthetic world (four coins,
  its window 2020-09 to 2025-03), with the world's own V3 pack in place of the fetched one. The
  real job gate runs on the world's own git repository, in four commits: the spec, the trial's
  registration, the code pin with the marker, and the registration files. Its V3 picks are a
  stand-in, because V3's four-year training is too long for a rehearsal.
  - It forces six stop outcomes through the real cell programs: `liquidated`,
    `limit_unrestorable`, `accounting_failure` and `engine_error` on `v4_main`, and `V3Stop` and
    `V3Accounting` on `v3_as_built`. Forcing refuses a world that is not a rehearsal's, and data
    that is not synthetic.
  - It judges eleven scenarios, the base set and ten altered ones. Each must get its expected
    verdict for its own reason: exactly the missing cells and refused folders its change implies,
    so a forced cell must be accepted. A base with another stopped `v4_main` cell shows as "NOT as
    expected": a false alarm to look into, never a silent pass.
  - Its artifacts carry rehearsal names, so a synthetic pass can never pass for the registered

## Why V4 exists: what the V3.1 run showed

V3.1's one registered run (run 38703911852, verdict FAIL, verdict.json SHA-256
`38e76612b712a7e064c3b21ef081d618701d17017f58e543db3d9d862510881d`) and the read-only
analysis of its 324 results (kept on the owner's PC) showed, for 2021-07 to 2024-12:

- every V3.1 component lost money. The combined system made -19% (10,000 USDT) and
  -25% (1,000 USDT), with worst drops up to 38%. It lost 6% even in the 2020-21 bull
  market (a window that was reported only);
- spot trend lost the most. Only 32% of trades won. Most trades ended at the initial
  stop, two four-hour ATRs away. The profit given back from trade peaks was about five
  times the final loss;
- "pullback" entries were the worst entry type, long and short. Short "continuation"
  entries in a confirmed downtrend were the only clearly profitable entries, mostly in
  2022;
- the grid lost in every year: target exits were wiped out by range-exit and stop
  losses, as in V1;
- the 30% lifetime shutdown fired in 9 of 12 combined cells in March-April 2023. The
  quarter-size recovery that followed lost more;
- switching the trailing stop off improved the result by about 9.5 points, from about
  -24.5% to about -15% at 10,000 USDT and from -25.0% to -15.9% at 1,000 USDT. It still
  lost money. This is corrected 2026-10-19 from complete cells, with the diagnostic replay
  of the crashed ones. An earlier "-19% to -4%" counted 3 cells that had crashed early.
  Removing automatic recovery helped by about 6 points; the other ablations helped less
  or not at all;
- on the same coins and dates, V3's daily long-only rules made +78% to +178%, with
  44-56% drops. Holding made +120% (an 81% drop). V3 as built (the walk-forward selector,
  long and short) made +25% to +28%, with 46-67% drops;
- 8 V3.1 cells at 10,000 USDT stopped early with
  `replay_failure:execution_failure:ValueError`. The root cause was found and fixed in
  PR #287 (2026-10-19): the routine cap check inside a settlement batch could cancel the
  very order being filled;
- the frozen V3.1 aggregate could not run at real scale. It needed 107 GB of disk, about
  75 GB of memory, and one to two weeks to rebuild every report.

V4 keeps what worked in V3 (slow daily signals, volatility sizing, holding until the
signal turns, no shutdown) and in V3.1 (one shared spot and futures account, the market
reading, detailed decision records, exact accounting, margin safety). It drops what the
evidence showed hurt.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-16/17, chat)

- **v4-d01.** Long and short in the main strategy. A long-only version runs alongside,
  reported only.
- **v4-d02.** No hard drawdown limit. The worst drop is reported and decides nothing.
- **v4-d03.** Test window: from the earliest data the project has to the latest complete
  month, then paper trading. This retires, for V4's data, the standing rule that market
  data from 2025-01-01 onwards is never read. Nothing unseen remains after this run, so
  forward paper trading becomes the final test.
- **v4-d04.** Pass rule: the four conditions in "Pass rule" below.
- **v4-d05.** Starting capitals 1,000 and 10,000 USDT. 100 USDT is dropped, because
  exchange minimums break a ten-coin strategy at that size.
- **v4-d06.** Exposure ceiling 3x equity: the most the project's rules allow in backtests
  and paper trading.
- **v4-d07.** "Best of both": a new V4 package that reuses proven parts of V3 and V3.1
  and modifies neither.
- **v4-d08.** Downtrends must be recognised sooner than a 12-month filter would. Shorts
  key on the medium speed plus a confirmed market reading.
- **v4-d09.** Include all seven additions: fewer trades, longs on spot first, funding on
  shorts, neighbor tests, robustness tests, a resumable verdict step, and coins chosen
  by a rule.
- **v4-d10 (2026-10-17, earlier).** Run results stay on the owner's PC. No public
  release.
- **v4-d11 (2026-10-20).** Delivery step 2 (universe and data) is built subagent-driven,
  each task reviewed, then the whole branch.
- **v4-d12 (2026-10-20, "yes").** Weak agreement between the speeds means smaller
  positions. Volatility scaling takes its scale from the full-strength book (see
  "Sizing"), so each coin's weight, before the caps of step 3, is |s| times its
  full-strength weight. It is a rule about each position, not a bound on the book's
  volatility.
- **v4-d13 (2026-10-20, "no no").** USDT symbols with non-ASCII names, such as
  币安人生USDT and 牛来USDT, stay excluded. The fetch allowlist is not widened.
- **v4-d14 (2026-10-21).** Delivery step 3 (the engine) is built subagent-driven.
- **v4-d15 (2026-10-21).** After step 3 merges, step 4 follows without waiting for a plan
  review. The work always stops before step 5's registered run.
- **v4-d16 (2026-10-22).** The universe stays the monthly top 10, with the rank-20 fallback
  for a dead candidate (see "Universe"). The owner considers coins outside the top 50
  unreliable or manipulated.
- **v4-d17 (2026-10-22, owner chose to keep it).** A universe coin whose spot venue has had
  no bar in the 168 hours before a decision leaves the sizing book: it is treated as signal
  0, so it is not in the common days, and it gets no target. A coin missing fewer hours stays
  in the book with its kept signal (see "Signals"). Without this, one delisted coin with a
  kept signal and no new returns drops the common days below 40 about 20 days later, and
  flattens every target until the next month.
- **v4-d18 (2026-10-22).** The cash clip stays as written. Only wallet cash funds increases;
  a winning short's unrealised profit does not.
- **v4-d19 (2026-10-22).** The long-only version may keep the dust-offset futures leg that
  the targets-net-of-dust rule produces.
- **v4-d20 (2026-10-22).** A reported-only comparison, `hold_btc_trend`, is added, built in
  step 4 (see "Versions in the run"). It brings the total to 40 cells. The pass rule is
  unchanged.

## Universe: coins chosen by a rule

- **Candidates.** Every Binance spot symbol quoted in USDT that ever traded, delisted
  ones included. Excluded:
  - stablecoins and fiat tokens: a fixed list (USDC, BUSD, TUSD, USDP, PAX, DAI, FDUSD,
    USDD, UST, EUR, GBP, AUD, TRY, BRL), plus any symbol whose daily close stayed within
    2% of 1.0 USDT on 90% of its last 90 days. This test is evaluated afresh at each
    selection date, over the 90 calendar days before that selection's 00:00 UTC. A day
    without a bar is not within the band, so at least 81 days must be. A pegged coin is
    therefore never judged on prices after its selection date;
  - leveraged tokens: base assets ending in UP, DOWN, BULL or BEAR, where the base without
    the suffix is itself the base of another listed USDT symbol (BTCUP, ETHBEAR), and a base
    that is exactly one of those words (BULL and BEAR, the BTC members of that family).
    Real coins like JUP are not caught;
  - wrapped or staked duplicates: WBTC, WBETH, BETH, STETH;
  - a symbol whose name is not made of ASCII capital letters and digits, such as
    币安人生USDT: its archives cannot be addressed under the fetch allowlist. Such symbols
    are recorded and counted, never dropped silently;
  - any symbol with fewer than 200 daily bars before the selection date;
  - any symbol with no daily bar in the 7 UTC days before the selection: a dead market,
    as in "Safety". So every ranked candidate has at least one volume observation.
- **Selection.** At 00:00 UTC on the first day of each month, rank the candidates by mean
  daily quote volume (USDT) over the previous 30 UTC days, using only days with a daily
  bar. Ties go alphabetically.
  - The universe takes candidates in rank order. A candidate is skipped if its hourly spot
    data has no unmasked bar in the 168 hours before the selection: it could neither
    update its signals nor fill, so it is a dead market for trading purposes.
  - The first 10 that are not skipped form the universe, considered down to rank 20 of
    the ranking (kept by v4-d16). So the data step fetches hourly spot for every symbol that
    ranks in the top 20 in any month.
  - While fewer than 10 candidates qualify, as in early 2018, or fewer than 10 of the top
    20 pass, the universe is all that qualify: a short universe is permitted.
- **Shorts, and the futures part of longs,** need an eligible USDT-margined perpetual: one
  listed at the decision time, in a funding month that is not incomplete (see "Data").
  Listed is judged point in time, never from later bars:
  - the perpetual's first hourly bar is at or before the decision;
  - it has had a bar within the 168 hours before the decision, so it is not a dead
    market (see "Safety");
  - the decision's own funding month is not incomplete. A status that is present and not
    `complete` makes it so. A month with no status, because the perpetual has no bars in
    it, counts as complete, and the 168-hour test alone says whether the perpetual is
    dead.

  Without such a perpetual, the coin is spot-only and long-only. A missing or masked
  perpetual bar at one decision or fill does not change this; the deferral rule alone
  handles it.
- **Leaving the top 10.** The month's selection is made at 00:00 UTC, before that same
  00:00 daily decision. That decision already uses the new universe, so a coin that left
  gets a target of 0 then, and its position fills out at the next hour's open.
- **The selection is recorded every month:** the ranked list, the volumes, and who
  entered and who left.

## Data

From Binance's public archive (`data.binance.vision`), exactly as earlier versions
fetched it, with the existing retry rules:

- daily spot klines for every candidate symbol. They are used for the universe only:
  ranking, the 200-bar history count and the stablecoin test. Signals and volatility use
  the daily bars aggregated from hourly spot bars (see "Signals"), which are the only
  source for every trading quantity;
- hourly spot klines for every symbol that ranks in the top 20 at least once, over its whole
  history from its first month. Signals, including the slower neighbor's 250-bar
  lookback, need bars from before the coin first enters;
- hourly USDT-M perpetual klines and funding-rate history for the same symbols' perpetuals
  where they exist, over their whole history. A coin X's perpetual is the futures symbol
  `XUSDT` if it has a monthly archive at or before the end month. Otherwise it is the one
  futures symbol, with such an archive, `P + X + USDT` whose
  prefix P is a contract multiplier: `1000`, `10000`, `100000`, `1000000` or `1M` (1,000,000),
  as in 1000SHIBUSDT or 1MBABYDOGEUSDT. With no such symbol, or more than one, the coin has
  no perpetual. A base that starts with `0` gets only its plain symbol: with a prefix, its
  name could be another coin's contract (`1000` + `0X` is `10000` + `X`).
  - One unit of a multiplier perpetual is that many coins, and its price is that many times
    the coin's price. The engine converts quantities and prices by the multiplier.
  - Exchange filters and funding are the perpetual's own, in its units.
  - The pack records each coin's perpetual and multiplier;
- present-day exchange filters, applied to history. They are disclosed as in V3 and V3.1:
  - tick and step;
  - the minimum and maximum quantity: the stricter of `LOT_SIZE` and `MARKET_LOT_SIZE`
    (the larger minimum, the smaller positive maximum), as V3.1's venue rules read them;
  - the minimum notional;
  - for each symbol and each venue, recorded and pinned.

  A symbol absent from the present-day snapshot, such as a delisted one, gets this fixed
  fallback, labelled `inferred` and pinned like the rest:
  - tick = 10^-d, where d is the most decimal places in any of the venue's hourly prices
    for that symbol, after trailing zeros are dropped;
  - step = 10^-d in the same way from its hourly base volumes, and minimum quantity =
    step;
  - maximum quantity = 9,000,000,000;
  - minimum notional = 5 USDT on either venue.

**Hourly bars are validated and masked** with V3's rules (V3 section 2), re-implemented in
V4 because the frozen readers refuse 2025 onward:
- the repairing reader of spec v1 section 5 rule 1 applies to both venues:
  - a duplicate or out-of-order row is dropped, and its hour and the last kept row's hour
    are masked;
  - a close off the hour boundary is repaired under the refined rule, and the repaired
    hour is masked, since no minute data checks it;
  - any other defective row is dropped and its hour masked;
  - an archive with a row whose open cannot be read is unreadable, and its whole month is
    masked;
- every missing hour between a symbol's first and last bar on that venue is masked. Hours
  before the first bar or after the last are not listed at all;
- a masked hour has no bar. It is not traded, positions are carried at the last mark (see
  "Safety"), and it counts towards the dead-market rule;
- a daily bar needs at least 20 unmasked hours of its UTC day (V3's per-day rule).
  Otherwise the day has no bar;
- daily spot archives, used only for the universe, go through the same reader. A masked
  or dropped day has no bar. So a duplicate or out-of-order daily row removes both its
  own day and the last kept row's day, as the reader's rule masks both.

V3's 17% rule, which excludes a whole coin-month, is not adopted for klines. With delisted
coins in the universe, it would drop a delisting month in advance and so foresee the
delisting. Masked hours only remove bars.

**Funding months are validated** with V3's exact schedule rule (V3 section 2):
- every record of the month declares the same interval I (`funding_interval_hours`), and I
  divides 24. A file without that column is read as I = 8;
- the expected slots are 00:00 UTC + k x I hours inside the month that fall within the
  perpetual's trading span: at or after the open of its first hourly bar, and at or before
  the close of its last, over its whole history. The span comes from the klines, never
  from the funding file being judged, so a file that loses its first or last records
  still fails. A listing or delisting month is therefore judged only while the perpetual
  traded, for the same reason as above;
- each record lies within 60 seconds after one slot, and every slot has exactly one record.

A missing file (inside that span), a missing slot, a duplicate, an off-slot record or a
change of interval makes the month incomplete. An incomplete month leaves the coin with no
eligible perpetual for the whole month, as V3's excluded coin-months work:
- no futures exposure is held in it. A futures position closes at the daily decision made
  at 00:00 UTC on the previous month's last day, after the close of the day before. That
  decision fills at 01:00 UTC that day, so the coin is flat in futures through every
  funding timestamp of the month. The close does not wait for a spot daily bar: it
  overrides the no-bar rule, as in V3;
- at the decision on the last day before a month, a coin whose next funding month is
  incomplete may not open futures, whether or not it holds any. A month with no funding
  status, because the perpetual has no bars in it, counts as complete, so a delisting is
  closed only by the dead-market rule. The rule does not apply when the next month is after
  the end month: the window-end close comes first;
- if that hour's perpetual bar is masked, the close moves to the next unmasked perpetual
  hour before the month starts, as in V3;
- if there is none, the leg closes at 00:00 UTC on the month's first day, before any of
  its funding. The close is synthetic, at the perpetual's last close with the 10%
  dead-market penalty and the venue's fee at the cell's own rate (see "Safety"), and
  ignores the filters. V3 would have invalidated the run; V4 books the penalised close
  instead;
- a long's target moves to spot under the spot-first rule, and a short's target is 0;
- futures may be used again from the first decision whose fill falls after the month.

Judging a month from its whole file is a look-ahead, disclosed below, as in V3. Funding is
charged exactly at the records' timestamps.

The window starts at 00:00 UTC on the first month whose selection has at least one
eligible candidate. Daily bars from 2017-08-17, when Binance spot USDT trading began, feed
the history, and every arm starts at that same boundary. The window ends at the last
complete calendar month before the prepare step runs; the exact end month is pinned in the
registration. Data preparation is pinned by SHA-256 like V3.1's packs. The hourly data is
one pack per coin, listed with its pin in a pinned index, so that no step holds every
coin's whole history at once. Fetch-code tests
must block the network.

## Signals: three speeds from daily closes

Daily bars are UTC days aggregated from hourly spot bars, as in V3 (section 2). Each
coin's signal is computed after each daily close, from completed bars only.

- **Fast:** up if close > SMA(20), down if close < SMA(20).
- **Medium:** the mean of two votes: EMA(21) vs EMA(55) (+1 above, -1 below), and V3's
  Donchian 55/20 state (+1, 0 or -1, V3 rule R3). Up if the mean is > 0, down if it is
  < 0.
- **Slow:** up if close > SMA(200), down if close < SMA(200).

A speed still warming up counts as neither up nor down. Every lookback counts available
daily bars, as V3 section 4 does ("n closes means the last n available bars"). A UTC day
without a daily bar adds no observation and resets nothing.

**A day with no daily bar for a coin** follows V3 section 4's no-bar rule. At the decision
after that day:
- the coin keeps its previous signal and takes no order, so its legs stay as they are;
- any deferred order it already has stays pending;
- the other coins are decided as usual, with its sigma and covariance entries using the
  returns it has.

A universe coin whose spot venue has had no bar in the 168 hours before the decision is a
dead market (see "Safety"), not a coin missing a day (v4-d17):
- it is treated as signal 0, so it leaves the sizing book and is not in the common days;
- it gets no target.

A coin missing fewer hours stays in the book with its kept signal, as above.

Two events override both rules, because they do not depend on the signal: a universe exit
and the funding-month close (see "Data"). Each is made even on a no-bar day, and each
cancels any deferred order the coin still has.

**Market reading, "confirmed downtrend":** all of
- close < SMA(50);
- SMA(50) is below its value 10 bars earlier;
- the high of the last 20 bars is below the high of the 20 bars before them (lower highs);
- the low of the last 20 bars is below the low of the 20 bars before them (lower lows).

**Short fraction S:**
- S = 0 unless the medium speed is down and the downtrend is confirmed;
- otherwise S = (0.5 if fast is down, else 0.25) x (2 if slow is down, else 1).

So medium and fast down gives 0.5; with slow also down, 1.0. A fast rally halves it.
The medium speed turning up closes it.

**Long fraction L:** the number of speeds that are up, divided by 3 (0, 1/3, 2/3 or 1).

**Net signal:** s = -S if S > 0, otherwise s = L. The long-only version uses s = L
always.

These lookbacks are standard settings fixed before any V4 result. They are not claimed
optimal.

## Sizing: volatility targeting, V3's method

At each daily decision, after day d's close, V3's sizing steps 1-3 (V3 spec section 5)
with V4's settings and one change in step 2:

1. raw weight = s x (1 / sigma), where sigma is the coin's annualised volatility over its
   last 60 daily returns. A coin with fewer than 60 returns, or with sigma = 0, gets 0,
   as in V3.
2. The scale comes from the full-strength book, so that agreement sets the size. Let
   `full` be each coin's `sign(s) x (1 / sigma)`, which is 0 where s = 0 or sigma is
   unavailable. Then:
   - `c = 0.40 / sqrt(full' Sigma full)`, with Sigma the 60-day covariance over common
     days, using the **volatility target: 40% a year** (main);
   - every weight is `c x raw weight`.

   So a book whose coins all have |s| = 1 runs at the 40% target, as V3's ±1 signals
   did. Each coin's weight is |s| times its full-strength weight, so L = 1/3 holds a
   third of the full position, and a fast rally halves a short. This is the difference from V3's step
   2, which scales the raw weights themselves and would undo the agreement sizing. If
   `full` is all 0, if `full' Sigma full` is 0, or if there are fewer than 40 common days,
   every target is 0. `full` is 0 for a coin whose raw weight is 0 under step 1.
3. Caps, in order: each coin |weight| <= 0.30; sum of |weights| <= 2.7 (90% of the 3x
   ceiling), scaled down proportionally if exceeded.

**Fewer trades:** a coin trades to its target only if the sign differs from the held
position, or the target is 0 with a position open, or
`|target - current| > max(0.25 x |target|, 0.01)`. Otherwise its position stays.

This test runs at the fill hour, exactly as V3 spec section 5 step 4 and section 6 run
theirs:
- `current` = the coin's signed notional over both venues (spot quantity x the spot
  open, plus futures quantity x the futures open, each at the fill hour's unslipped
  open), divided by equity at that open before the hour's fills;
- the target quantities use the same opens and equity (V3 section 5 step 5);
- if any venue the coin's order needs has a masked or missing fill-hour bar, the coin's
  whole order is deferred under V3's masked-fill-hour rules, including colliding
  decisions and the 00:00 rule;
- the exception is a mandatory exit, which does not depend on the signal: a universe
  exit or a funding-month exit. It closes each leg on that leg's own venue as soon as
  that venue has a bar, independently of the other venue. After a funding-month close of
  either side, a replacement spot buy is made only when the coin's latest target is long.
  It waits for that futures close: it is placed at the
  first hour at or after the close's fill that has a spot bar, and is sized there. If
  that hour is a 00:00 decision hour, and the decision made then produces a newer target
  for the coin, that decision supersedes it, as V3's 00:00 rule does: the replacement is
  cancelled, and only the new decision's order fills, at 01:00. A no-bar decision produces
  no newer target, so the replacement stays pending and fills as placed. So only the
  latest target ever produces the buy.

## Account and execution: one shared account

- **One wallet** holds USDT cash, spot coins and futures collateral. Equity is cash plus
  spot coins at mark, plus futures collateral, plus futures unrealized profit or loss.
  Money is counted once, as in V3.1 (Package D).
- **Longs on spot first, coin by coin.** All amounts are in USDT, from the targets at the
  fill hour.
  - **Deferrals come first,** before any allocation, and the allocation is computed once,
    never again after it. A coin's order is deferred when a venue it needs lacks the
    fill-hour bar:
    - it needs spot if it holds spot or its target is long;
    - it needs the perpetual if it holds futures, its target is short, or it is long with
      an eligible perpetual;
    - an order that offsets the coin's dust with a futures leg, a target of 0 included,
      needs the perpetual too, like any futures order.

    A deferred coin, a no-bar coin and a coin the band test leaves alone all keep their
    legs as held. A leg that a mandatory exit closes this hour counts as 0, since it
    fills in the same batch's reductions. The coin's other, deferred leg stays as held.
  - **Fixed holdings.** The spot notional of the coins that keep their legs, dust
    excluded, sums into S_K, and their futures notional, long or short, into F_K. The
    unsellable dust of every coin, trading or not, sums into D. None of these is
    re-split.
  - **Targets net of dust.** Each trading coin's target notional is first reduced by its
    own dust: `residual = target - dust` (signed).
    - A positive residual is a long and a negative one a short.
    - A spot-only coin's negative residual is treated as 0: it holds its dust and
      nothing else.
    - The split below runs on the residuals. So the futures notional that offsets dust
      is inside G_S or G_E, and the dust itself is inside D.
    - The long-only version follows the same rule, so it may hold a futures short that
      offsets dust (v4-d19).
  - **Definitions,** over the coins that trade this hour, which are all the others. E is
    the set of coins with a positive residual and an eligible perpetual, and N the
    spot-only coins with a positive residual. Their residuals sum to G_E and G_N, and
    G_L = G_E + G_N. The negative residuals' absolute values sum to G_S, all on futures.
    Futures need initial margin of notional / 3, and 2% of equity is held back for
    costs. The room left is `R = 0.98 x equity - S_K - D - F_K / 3`.
  - **Orders from the split.** A trading coin's spot order is its spot share of the
    residual minus its current spot, dust excluded, and its futures order moves its
    futures leg to its futures share. A sale that would fall below the filters is not
    made, and the remainder joins its dust.
  - **Spot capacity:** `s_max = max(0, min(G_L, 1.5 x (R - (G_L + G_S) / 3)))`.
  - **If `s_max >= G_N`:**
    - every N coin is held fully on spot;
    - the remaining spot capacity `s_max - G_N` goes to the E coins pro rata to their
      targets;
    - each E coin's rest is on futures.
  - **If `s_max < G_N`:**
    - every E coin is held fully on futures;
    - every N coin's target is scaled by one common factor
      `k = max(0, R - (G_E + G_S) / 3) / G_N`, capped at 1, and held on spot;
    - nothing is redistributed to other coins.
  - **Cash clip,** as V3's hold benchmark does for spot. The fills go reductions first,
    then increases, each alphabetical by symbol; within one coin, the spot leg goes before
    the futures leg. An increase that the free cash cannot pay for at
    its slipped price is cut to what the free cash pays for, rounded down to the step. A
    spot buy needs its notional plus its fee; a futures increase needs its initial margin
    plus its fee. Free cash is the smaller of:
    - wallet USDT minus the initial margin of the futures positions then open;
    - backing minus 4 x maintenance (see "Safety").

    So no increase, spot or futures, takes the margin ratio below 4, and none is made
    while it is below 4. An increase cut below the filters is skipped and recorded.

    Increases are admitted one at a time, each on the account as it stands after the
    previous real fill. An increase that rounding or the filters skip is not a fill. Only
    wallet cash funds an increase: a winning short's unrealised profit does not (v4-d18).
- **Shorts on futures.** Historical funding is charged to and paid out of the wallet.
  Longs on futures pay it, and shorts are paid it when the rate is positive. Each payment
  is quantity x the published rate x the perpetual's price, as in V3: the open of the
  settlement hour, or the carried mark when that hour is masked.
- **Timing:** decisions after the daily close (00:00 UTC). Orders fill at the next hour's
  open with slippage, as in V3.
- **Costs (normal):** spot fee 0.10%, futures fee 0.05%, slippage 0.05% per fill. These are
  the V3.1 cost profile. Doubled costs double all three.
- **Exchange filters,** following V3 spec section 5 step 5:
  - quantities round toward zero to the step;
  - an order that opens or increases a position is not made if it is below the minimum
    quantity or the minimum notional, and the refusal is recorded;
  - an order above the maximum quantity is split into the fewest orders, each paying its
    fee.
- **Reductions are never refused for size:**
  - a futures reduction (a signal close, universe exit, margin cut, ceiling cut,
    funding-month exit or delisting) is reduce-only, so it ignores the minimum notional. A reduction below the
    minimum quantity is raised to it, or to the whole position. One that would leave less
    than the minimum quantity closes the whole position (V3 section 5 step 5);
  - a spot remainder that cannot be sold because it is below the minimum quantity or the
    minimum notional stays as dust: valued at its mark, reported, and sold as soon as it
    can be (V3's dust rule, as in V3.1). That is at the coin's next order once it can be
    sold; for a hold comparison, its next monthly rebalance.
- **Order of events in an hour:** exactly V3 spec section 6, "Order of events in an
  hour", with one addition placed first:
  - the dead-market closes due at this hour (see "Safety") and any synthetic
    funding-month close due at this instant (see "Data"), booked at the hour's open time
    before anything else. Funding records at or after that instant are not charged on a
    closed leg;
  - the open mark, then the pre-fill liquidation check;
  - the daily fills at the open (reductions first, then increases, each alphabetical);
  - the post-fill mark with the liquidation check, then the margin-cushion check, then the
    leverage-limit check, which is V4's 3x ceiling. Each cut fills at the open and is
    followed by a new mark;
  - funding after the hour's trading, on the positions then held. After each funding
    event come a mark, the liquidation check, the cushion check and the leverage-limit
    check again;
  - the liquidation check at the bar's adverse extremes, all at once.

  As in V3, an excursion inside an hour is not a ceiling breach, and every cut fills at
  the open. V4 places no order inside an hour, so no intrabar price ordering exists that
  could change a result.
- **Window end.** V4's arms and the `hold_*` comparisons close every open position at the
  last hour's close with that arm's fees and slippage. They are the cell's own fee and
  slippage rates, so doubled-cost cells pay them doubled. The exit reason is "window end",
  and final equity is taken after those fills.
  - A venue without a bar in the last hour closes at its last close, the carried mark.
  - A spot remainder that is still unsellable dust stays valued at that close, with its
    lifecycle censored as "window end (dust)". No synthetic sale is made.
  - `v3_as_built` keeps V3's own ending: positions marked at its last close, censored
    lifecycles and no exit fill (V3 sections 6 and 8). It is the unchanged comparator.

## Safety

- **No tight stop-losses, no trailing stop, no lifetime shutdown, no recovery mode.**
  Positions change only through the signals, the caps and the rules below.
- **The 3x ceiling** is V3's leverage-limit check with m = 3, where gross exposure counts
  spot and futures notional: `(sum |spot notional| + sum |futures notional|) / equity`. It
  runs every hour at the post-fill mark and after funding (V3 section 6). Above 3.0, every
  position is cut pro rata to 2.7 by reduce-only orders at that open, with slippage and
  fees. V4's target of 2.7 replaces V3's 0.80 x m, and spot legs are cut by selling spot.
  - **Positions that cannot trade,** because their hour is masked or they are unsellable
    spot dust, stay at their carried marks. Their gross is the untradable gross. The
    tradable positions are cut by V3's factor
    `k' = (2.7 x equity - untradable gross) / tradable gross`. If k' <= 0, every tradable
    position is closed. A spot remainder a cut leaves below the filters becomes dust.
  - **The limit cannot be restored** if no position can trade (tradable gross is 0), or if
    gross exposure still exceeds 3.0 at the new post-fill mark after a cut. Then the cell
    ends with outcome `limit_unrestorable` (see "Reports"). So does a fill the rules
    require that cannot be made. As in V3 section 8, these are strategy outcomes and fail
    acceptance, not engine errors.
  - **A required sale the filters refuse.** A cut's spot sale below the minimum quantity or
    notional, of a holding that can itself be sold, is not made: the leg stays held, not
    dust. If the limit is still breached after that cut, the outcome is
    `limit_unrestorable` with reason `required_fill`.
- **Futures margin** follows V3.1's shared-account model (`futures_margin` in
  `combined/execution.py`):
  - backing is free cash, plus futures collateral, plus futures unrealised profit or loss.
    Spot inventory does not back futures;
  - maintenance is 1% of futures notional, as in V3;
  - the margin ratio is backing / maintenance. With no futures position, maintenance is
    0 and the ratio is +infinity, so neither the cushion nor the record divides by zero.
- **Margin cushion** (the owner-approved safety rule, now on that backing):
  - **Admission:** the cash clip (see "Account and execution") caps every increase,
    spot or futures, so that the ratio stays at least 4 after its fee.
  - **Cut:** at each point where the 3x ceiling is checked, and just before it, a ratio
    below 3 cuts the tradable futures positions pro rata, by reduce-only orders at that
    open, by `k = (backing / 4 - untradable maintenance) / tradable maintenance`. This
    brings the ratio back to 4 before fees. If k <= 0, every tradable futures position
    is closed. The 3x ceiling check then runs on the new mark.
  - **The cushion cannot be restored** if the ratio is below 3 and no futures position
    can trade, or if it is still below 3 at the new mark after a cut. Then the cell ends
    with outcome `limit_unrestorable`, reason `margin_cushion`, as for the 3x ceiling.
- **Liquidation:** V3's check on that backing, at the open and at the bar's adverse
  extremes, as V3 section 6 does. Futures legs are taken at their adverse extremes at once
  (a long's low, a short's high). The cell is liquidated when backing is at or below
  maintenance. A liquidation ends the cell, is recorded, and fails acceptance.
- **How a cell that stops early ends,** after a liquidation or an unrestorable limit, as
  V3 section 8 ends a run:
  - no later event is simulated, and no closing fill, fee or slippage is charged;
  - every open position, dust included, is marked at the prices of the check that
    stopped it. Masked legs use their carried marks. When the adverse-extremes check
    stopped the cell, that mark takes spot legs at their bar lows too;
  - each lifecycle is censored, with the reason (`liquidation` or `limit_unrestorable`)
    and its unrealised profit or loss;
  - that mark is the cell's final equity.
- **Missing or stale data:** a coin without a fresh bar is not traded. An open position is
  carried at its last mark (V3's masked-hour handling) until data returns or the
  dead-market rule below closes it.
- **Delisting or a dead market:** once a held position's own venue has had no bar for 168
  consecutive hours (7 days, masked hours included), that position closes at the open time
  of the next hour, whether or not that hour has a bar. It closes at its venue's last
  close with a 10% adverse price penalty: below it for longs, above it for shorts. The
  close is the first event of that hour (see "Order of events").
  - The venue is spot for a spot leg and the perpetual for a futures leg, so a perpetual
    that dies while spot still trades is handled on its own.
  - No funding is charged for hours without a funding record.
  - The close is synthetic: it ignores the exchange filters, so a dust remainder closes
    with the rest of the leg, at the same penalised price. The penalty replaces
    slippage, and the venue's fee is charged on the penalised notional. The fee is the
    cell's own fee rate, so doubled-cost cells pay it doubled.
  - **Solvency at the penalised price.** All synthetic closes due at the same instant,
    dead-market or funding-month, on any venue, form one batch.
    - The liquidation check runs once, with every closing leg valued at its penalised
      price at the same time and every other leg at its mark. A liquidation there ends
      the cell.
    - Otherwise every close in the batch is booked together, so the outcome never
      depends on the order in which the closes are processed.
    - After the batch, equity at or below zero also ends the cell as liquidated.
  - **Pending orders are cancelled.** A dead-market close cancels any deferred order the
    coin still has. The coin re-enters only through a later daily decision whose
    decision day has a bar, never through an order made before the close.
  - The rule uses only what was known at the time, never whether data later resumes. The
    penalty is deliberately against the strategy. A coin whose data resumes can enter
    again through the normal rules, as above.
  - **A dead market and a funding-month close at the same midnight:** the leg closes
    once, as a delisting, and no replacement buy is made.
  - **A universe coin whose spot is dead while its perpetual still trades** gets no
    target. A futures leg it holds is then managed only by the margin and ceiling rules
    until the coin leaves the universe.
- **Accounting:** wallet and quantity identities exact. Equity-change residual
  <= 1e-18 USDT, kept and reported. A larger residual is an engine failure.

**The hold comparisons are spot accounts** (`hold_universe`, `hold_btc` and, below,
`hold_btc_trend`) with V3's hold-benchmark spot rules (V3 section 8): the spot fee and
V4's slippage, the spot filters above, no borrowing, no funding, and no ceiling or
liquidation check. A buy spends its notional plus its fee from cash, a sell adds its
notional minus its fee, and dust follows the dust rule above.
- `hold_universe` rebalances at each month's first daily decision and fills at that day's
  01:00 open, as V4's orders do:
  - a month with an empty universe gives every coin a target of 0: the account sells
    what it can and holds cash and dust;
  - with n >= 1 coins in the month's universe, each gets a target of
    `0.99 x equity / n`, using
    equity at that open before the fills. The other 1% stays in cash for fees and
    slippage;
  - a coin that left the universe gets a target of 0;
  - there is no band: every coin trades to its target each month, subject to the filters;
  - sells go before buys, each group in alphabetical order. A buy larger than the cash then
    available is cut to what the cash pays for, fee included, and rounded down to the step;
  - an order below the minimum quantity or notional is skipped and recorded, and a sell's
    unsellable remainder is dust;
  - masked fill hours, the dead-market rule and the window end apply as for V4.
- `hold_btc` buys BTCUSDT at the window's first fill hour with `0.99 x equity`, under the
  same cash rule, and holds it to the window end.

**`hold_btc_trend`** (v4-d20, built in step 4) is reported only and decides nothing. It is a

rule, dust, masked fill hours (deferral), the dead-market rule and the window end.
- It holds BTCUSDT with `0.99 x equity` while BTC's daily close is above its SMA(200), and
  all cash otherwise.
- The test runs at 00:00 from completed daily bars, exactly as V4's slow speed does, and
  the order fills at 01:00.
- It trades only when that state changes. A warming SMA counts as cash.
- A day with no BTC daily bar keeps the state. An order that never fills (refused below the
  filters, or cancelled or closed by the dead-market rule) is not made again until the vote
  changes.

Each version runs at 2 capitals x 2 cost levels = 4 cells, comparisons included. The total
is 12 x 4 = 48 cells (10 x 4 = 40 before v4-d22 added two versions). The exact rules of the two
v4-d22 versions and of `v3_as_built` are in "Amended in the V4 step-4a PR" above.

The two price paths (high first, low first) of the first draft are dropped. V4 trades only
at hour opens and checks liquidation at all adverse extremes at once (V3 section 6), so
both paths would produce identical results.

**The neighbors scale only these signal lookbacks:**
- fast SMA 20 -> 15/25;
- medium EMA 21 and 55 -> 16/26 and 41/69;
- Donchian 55/20 -> 41/15 and 69/25;
- slow SMA 200 -> 150/250;
- the downtrend's SMA 50 -> 38/62, its 10-day slope -> 8/12, and its 20-day highs and
  lows -> 15/25.

Everything else stays fixed, so the neighbors trade the same universe with the same
sizing:
- the universe's 200-bar history minimum, 30-day volume mean and 90-day stablecoin test;
- the 60-day volatility, the 60-day covariance and its 40-common-day minimum.

`v3_as_built` runs from the first day V3's own pipeline can trade. V3's frozen code
refuses data after 2024-12, so it ends there. Comparisons with it use the common period.
- At 10,000 USDT it runs exactly as built.
- At 1,000 USDT it runs through V3.1's reviewed capital adapter, which changes only the
  starting capital (disclosed, as in V3.1's `unchanged_v3_*` cells). Fills that the
  exchange filters change at the smaller size are part of that disclosure.

## Pass rule (v4-d04)

V4.0 passes only if `v4_main` meets all four, at both capitals:

1. its compounded return / maximum drawdown is greater than `hold_universe`'s (normal
   costs);
2. its compounded return is greater than `v3_as_built`'s over their common period
   (normal costs);
3. its net profit is positive at doubled costs;
4. its equity at the window's end is greater than its equity at 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC
   (normal costs).

**One common measure for every arm.** Compounded return and maximum drawdown in
conditions 1 and 2 use each arm's equity marked at the common hourly boundaries: the open
of every hour at which every price source the compared arms use has its bar.
- The mark is the open mark of the order of events: after any synthetic close booked
  at that hour's open, and before the hour's daily fills.
- Completeness is judged on the instruments the arm holds or trades at that mark: an arm
  uses a price source at an hour when it holds or trades that instrument then.
- This follows V3.1's comparison policy. Hours missing from any source are skipped for
  all arms alike and listed. Intrabar extremes drive the liquidation check, but never
  this measure.
- **Condition 1** (`v4_main` and `hold_universe`, the same window): the path is the common
  boundaries, then each arm's final equity after its window-end close, as the last point.
  Both arms close at the same hour with costs.
- **Condition 2** (`v4_main` and `v3_as_built`): the path is the common boundaries from
  `v3_as_built`'s first trading hour to the last hour open in its period, 2024-12-31 23:00
  UTC. Neither arm has a synthetic close here: V4 is still trading, and V3's terminal mark
  is not on an hour open.
- **Conditions 3 and 4** use final equity after the window-end close. Condition 4 compares
  it with equity at the open of 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC.
- **Zero drawdown,** as V3 section 8 treats its Calmar ratio. Return / maximum drawdown is
  +infinity when the drawdown is 0 and the return is positive, and 0 when the drawdown is
  0 and the return is 0 or less. +infinity is greater than every finite value and not
  greater than itself.

**Precedence:** a liquidation, an unrestorable limit or an accounting failure in any
`v4_main` cell means fail, whatever else is missing. Otherwise, anything missing means incomplete, never a pass. Each
cell's `summary.json` states its outcome (see "Reports"), so the verdict can tell these
cases apart from the summaries alone.
Maximum drawdown, CAGR, Sharpe and per-era and per-year results are reported only.

**Eras reported separately:** the window's start to 2020-12-31, 2021-01-01 to
2024-12-31, and 2025-01-01 to the end. The report states plainly that only the third era was unseen when
V4 was designed, so a pass means "promising", not "proven".

## Records

- **Daily, per coin in or leaving the universe:**
  - each speed's values and vote, the downtrend reading and its four parts;
  - L, S and s, sigma, and the raw weight;
  - the weight after each step (volatility scaling, coin cap, total cap, band), naming
    the step that changed it, and the spot and futures split;
  - the order placed or the reason none was;
  - fills, fees and funding;
  - for the two v4-d22 arms, two more fields on every `daily_coin` record, null for every
    other arm:
    - `short_filter`, for an arm with the filter (`v4_btc200_shorts`):
      `{"btc_slow_vote": 1|-1|null, "allowed": bool}`, BTC's latest slow vote (null while its
      SMA(200) is warming up, on an exact tie, or before its first bar) and whether shorts
      were allowed at that decision;
    - `confirm`, for an arm with `confirm_readings` (`v4_confirm3`):
      `{"direction", "run", "effective"}`, the coin's latest reading's direction (+1, 0 or
      -1), that direction's run of consecutive readings and the effective signal, all 0
      before its first reading.

  Each coin-day has a decision record at 00:00 (the signals, the sizing steps and the
  target, or why there is none). When its order comes due, an order record at the fill hour
  holds the band result, the split and the orders, or the reason none was made, since the
  band and the split run there.
- **Per trade lifecycle:** entry and exit, exit reason (signal, universe exit,
  funding-month exit, margin cut, ceiling cut, delisting, window end, window end (dust)),
  gross P&L, fees, funding, best unrealized profit, profit given back, holding time.
  - A lifecycle is per coin and side. It opens when the coin's signed position over both
    venues leaves zero, and ends when it changes sign, or when both legs are flat. Dust
    keeps a lifecycle open until it is sold or censored at the window end, so a later
    dust sale, its fee, profit or loss and the holding time stay inside it. A futures
    leg that offsets dust also keeps it open. Both tests run
    after a whole batch of fills (the hour's daily fills, or one cut), never between
    the fills of a batch. So a full spot-to-futures move stays one lifecycle.
  - Moving part of a long between spot and futures stays inside one lifecycle. The record
    lists each venue's fills, fees and funding, and names the coin's perpetual and its
    multiplier.
  - Best unrealized profit and profit given back are measured on the combined position.
    Best unrealized profit is the maximum, over the lifecycle's hourly open marks and its
    final gross P&L, of gross P&L so far plus unrealized P&L. Profit given back is that best
    minus the final gross P&L, never negative.
- **Monthly:** the universe selection record and each perpetual's funding-month validation.
- **Hourly:** equity, gross exposure, margin ratio (backing over maintenance).
- **A `v3_as_built` cell** has V3's own marks in the same chain, and none of the engine's
  daily, fill, lifecycle, monthly or funding records:
  - an `hourly` record per hour open, `{open_ms, open_equity, complete}`, from V3's `open`
    native mark;
  - V3's terminal mark as an `event` of type `terminal`
    (`{type, hour_ms, coin: null, timestamp_ms, equity}`, at 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC). A run
    that reached its window's end has exactly one, and a liquidation has none;
  - the `outcome` record, last.

  Its summary's format is `v4-summary-v3-1` (see "Amended in the V4 step-4a PR"): `data` is
  the two pins, and `accounting_residual` is V3's own reconciliation residual, not V4's
  hourly audit residual.
- **Outcome,** last: the cell's outcome record names its cell (arm, capital, cost level),
  its window and the data pins. The summary refuses records of another cell, another window
  or another capital.
- **Integrity:** all records hash-chained, in V3.1's `combined-v1` style with a V4 schema
  name. Each cell's bundle carries a receipt.

## Reports

Per cell: an HTML page and a `summary.json` holding exactly the numbers acceptance needs.
For `v3_as_built`, the cell builds it from V3's own records without changing V3's code.

Every cell that runs writes a `summary.json`, even one that stops early. A cell that
cannot run at all, or whose summary fails its self-verification, writes no `summary.json`,
and the verdict counts it as incomplete. A written summary's `outcome` field is one of:
- `complete`, in a full summary;
- `liquidated`, with the hour, in a full summary up to that hour;
- `limit_unrestorable`, with the hour and the reason (`ceiling` for the 3x ceiling,
  `margin_cushion`, or `required_fill` for a required fill that cannot be made), in a full
  summary up to that hour;
- `accounting_failure`, with the identity that failed (wallet, quantity or equity
  residual), its hour and the values, in a short failure summary;
- `engine_error`, with the exception's type and message, in a short failure summary. For
  `v3_as_built` the type is `V3Stop` (a V3 stop other than a liquidation) or `V3Accounting`
  (a completed or liquidated V3 run whose own accounting flags are not true), as "Amended in
  the V4 step-4a PR" says. Both are incomplete, so condition 2 of the pass rule is then
  incomplete.

Every summary, full or short, names its cell, its window and the data pins, as the outcome
record does (see "Records").

The verdict treats `liquidated`, `limit_unrestorable` and `accounting_failure` in
`v4_main` as fail. It treats
`engine_error`, a missing summary or a missing field as incomplete. The outcome is read
first and takes precedence. In a `liquidated` or `limit_unrestorable` summary, equity at
2025-01-01 is null when the stop came earlier. Final equity after the window-end close
is always null there, because no closing fill happens. The final equity is the stop's
mark (see "Safety"), and the hourly marks run to the stop. None of these nulls makes
the cell incomplete. A full summary holds:
- final and initial equity, maximum drawdown, compounded return;
- equity at 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC, and final equity after the window-end close;
- the open mark at every hour of the cell (before that hour's fills), each flagged
  "complete" when every price source the arm uses then has that hour's bar. From these the
  verdict computes the common hourly boundaries of any pair of arms (the hours complete in
  both) and every comparison above, without reading the full reports. At about 70,000
  hours a cell, this is a few megabytes;
- per-era and per-year returns, liquidations, accounting residual;
- the bundle receipt SHA-256.

Run summary: the verdict, the comparison table, per-era results, long vs short, per coin,
neighbors and volatility variants, and a plain-language page.

  V3.1's shared-account ledger, cost, funding, margin and record schema. Copy into `v4/`
  where it does not. Originals are not changed to suit V4. The one exception is a reviewed
  bug fix with regression tests in non-frozen shared code, as in PR #287. Frozen
  directories are never edited.
- **The V3.1 crash first.** Done 2026-10-19. The root cause of
  `replay_failure:execution_failure:ValueError` is fixed in `combined/engine.py` with
  regression tests (PR #287, follow-ups #288). It is a bug fix in non-frozen shared code

- **Each cell verifies its own report** (rebuild and equality, as V3.1's

- **The verdict step:**
  - reads only the `summary.json` files and receipts, one cell at a time;
  - keeps a progress file and resumes after an interruption;
  - must not need more than 2 GB of memory or 5 GB of disk;

  independent, timestamped provenance. Its artifacts are downloaded to the owner's PC and
  checked against GitHub's digests. Deleting them from GitHub afterwards is offered to

## Known limitations

- 2017-2024 was studied before V4 was designed. Only 2025 onwards was not.
- Present-day exchange filters are applied to history. Delisted symbols, which have none,
  use a fixed fallback: tick and step from their own decimals, minimum notional 5 USDT.
- USDT symbols with non-ASCII names, such as 币安人生USDT and 牛来USDT, are excluded,
  because the fetch allowlist addresses only ASCII capital letters and digits. The owner
  decided to keep them out and not widen the allowlist (v4-d13).
- A perpetual's expected funding slots start at the first slot at or after its first
  hourly bar. One listed exactly on a slot hour, whose first funding comes a slot later,
  makes its listing month incomplete, so futures wait until the next month. This is
  conservative.
- A perpetual's funding month is judged complete or incomplete from its whole file, and
  futures leave the day before it starts: a look-ahead of up to a month and a day. This is
  V3's treatment of excluded coin-months, and it affects only whether futures may be used
  that month.
- The 10% delisting penalty is an assumption.
- Coin selection by Binance volume is a proxy for size. It still omits coins never
  listed on Binance.
- Funding history may start later than futures prices for some symbols. Such a coin is
  long-only until both exist.
- 3x exposure can produce large drops. The owner accepted this (v4-d02, v4-d06).
