## 3. Corrections our documents need (most important first)

1. **Withdraw arXiv 2608.21888 as evidence and record the exposure.** Its primary sample is Jan 2025 – Feb 2026, with a holdout to Aug 2026, which is the reserved window. That covers both the "90% of 183 pairs" and the "about 1.3 bp" figures. The same applies to the repo's earlier summary (`2026-09-24-claude-fees-and-strategy-plan.md` line 97). Also withdraw the IsaacDodds GitHub "halved drawdown" claim, because its backtest runs to 2026-05. The qualitative point (the grid has no edge after costs) can stand on arXiv 2506.11921's random-walk/fees argument (A14).
2. **Binance access needs re-checking with the owner.** Binance lost its EU standing on 1 Jul 2026 for lack of a MiCA licence, and HANFA's statement applies to Croatia. The owner's "I have access" and "both Binance and Kraken" may be out of date for new positions. Binance archives remain usable as research data, but Binance as the **venue** is doubtful. Kraken (CySEC, MiFID II) is currently the one verified EEA route (B6–B9).
3. **BIS "Crypto carry" is misdescribed.** It measures the dated-futures basis from 2019 to Jan 2022, not perpetual funding. Three statements in notes §3.5 have no source: "in bear markets funding is often negative", "sharply negative in March 2020", and funding as "positive, mostly in bull markets". Re-source them or mark them unverified. What BIS does support: average carry of about 10% p.a., boom-driven spikes, high carry predicting crashes, and liquidation risk at leverage. The assessment's "3–7% vs BIS" comparison also mixes instruments.
4. **Daniel & Moskowitz, "roughly halves the worst drawdowns": not in the source.** Replace it with this: dynamic volatility scaling roughly doubles the Sharpe ratio of equity momentum; constant-volatility scaling improves it less; the gains come partly from avoiding crashes. Barroso & Santa-Clara (2015) say risk management "virtually eliminates crashes". State the 2009 loss precisely: WML −42% in March and −46% in April, about −69% over two months (corrected 2026-09-28 from −74%), for a zero-cost long/short portfolio.
5. **Kraken EEA facts to add, and a timing trap.** The facts: hourly funding (not 8-hourly), 0.0001 BTC minimum lot, up to 10× leverage, base fees of 0.02% maker and 0.05% taker, and an appropriateness test. The trap: the EEA product has existed only since about 2025, so its own history is all in the reserved window. Kraken EEA verification must therefore be prospective paper trading, or use pre-2025 Kraken Futures history explicitly labelled as a different entity.
6. **arXiv 2602.11708 is 70/30 long/short on 6-hour bars, not long-only.** It is a non-reviewed preprint, and its figure extends into Oct 2025. The "including long-only" claim should rest on Le & Ruthbah alone, which has been read at abstract depth only.
7. **§4.4 data feasibility, now answered.** The Binance kline, funding, mark and index archives for BTCUSDT start in **2020-01**, not Sep 2019. Only trades go back to 2019-09. Open interest (metrics) starts on **2020-09-01**. No archive covers 2018. A perpetual-based test therefore starts in 2020 at the earliest, and in 2020-09 if it uses open interest.
8. **Moreira & Muir:** add the Cederburg et al. (2020) out-of-sample caveat. Across 103 strategies, realistic volatility management generally did not beat the unmanaged portfolios.
9. **Minor.** Hurst et al.: say "positive returns in 8 of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns", net of simulated costs and 2/20 fees. Assessment: USDC's low was about $0.87, not $0.88. The report's "mid-2024 stayed above the 200-day" is contradicted for Aug 2024 (A24).
